# Healthy Coping Strategies and Grounding Techniques

When facing difficult emotions, deliberate coping strategies help activate the parasympathetic nervous system and restore a sense of calm.

## 1. Grounding: The 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Exercise
A proven mindfulness technique to anchor you in the present moment:
- 5: Acknowledge FIVE things you can see around you (e.g., a pen, light through a window, a cup).
- 4: Acknowledge FOUR things you can touch or feel (e.g., your feet on the floor, the texture of your shirt).
- 3: Acknowledge THREE things you can hear (e.g., a clock ticking, distant traffic, your own breath).
- 2: Acknowledge TWO things you can smell (e.g., fresh air, coffee, fabric).
- 1: Acknowledge ONE thing you can taste (or take a gentle sip of cool water).

## 2. Physiological Sigh and Box Breathing
- Physiological Sigh: Inhale deeply through the nose, take a short second 'top-up' inhale, and exhale slowly and fully through the mouth. Repeating this 2-3 times rapidly reduces autonomic arousal.
- Box Breathing (Square Breathing): Inhale for 4 counts, hold gently for 4 counts, exhale smoothly for 4 counts, and hold empty for 4 counts.

## 3. Cognitive Reframing and Micro-Pacing
- The "Two-Minute Next Step": When feeling paralyzed by a massive workload or exam revision, identify one microscopic task you can complete in under two minutes (e.g., opening a document or writing a single sentence).
- Naming the Feeling: Neurological studies demonstrate that silently labeling an emotion ("I am feeling anxious right now") down-regulates amygdala hyperactivity.
- Compassionate Self-Talk: Treat yourself with the same warmth, empathy, and patience that you would extend to a close friend in distress.
